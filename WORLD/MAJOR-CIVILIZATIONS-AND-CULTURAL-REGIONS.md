# WORLD — MAJOR CIVILIZATIONS & CULTURAL REGIONS

> Status: PROVISIONAL
> Purpose: turn the species/geography foundations into a living political and cultural world.
> Core rule: civilizations are not species containers. They are historical societies made by geography, migration, conflict, trade, belief and ordinary people.

---

# 1. CIVILIZATION DESIGN LAW

A civilization is not:
- one race;
- one personality;
- one aesthetic;
- one government;
- one moral position.

Every major civilization should contain:
- different classes;
- internal political factions;
- multiple ethnic/species communities;
- rural and urban life;
- rich and poor districts;
- old traditions and new movements;
- ordinary people who do not care about grand history;
- people who benefit from the current system;
- people harmed by it;
- people trying to reform it;
- people trying to destroy it.

A civilization can be beautiful and unjust at the same time.

A government can protect people in one area and oppress them in another.

A revolutionary can be right about one problem and dangerously wrong about another.

This complexity should exist without turning every arc into political exposition.

# 2. THE NINE MAJOR CIVILIZATIONAL ZONES

These are broad current-era powers/regions, not the only countries in the world.

Their names are provisional.

---

## I. THE ARCADIAN COMMONWEALTH — THE MANY-CITY REPUBLICS

### Region
Westerly Sea / Central Continent.

### Political structure
A federation of major cities, surrounding towns and agricultural territories.

No single city completely controls the others.

Each member city has local law, while the Commonwealth controls:
- shared trade rules;
- currency standards;
- interstate roads;
- major courts;
- collective defense.

### Why it exists

Centuries ago, several rival cities nearly destroyed the region through trade wars.

They eventually discovered that shared infrastructure was more profitable than constant conflict.

The Commonwealth was born from practical cooperation rather than idealism.

### Culture

Arcadian people are proud of civic participation.

Public squares are important.

Arguments are normal.

Political debates can become enormous public events.

Children sometimes attend local council meetings as school assignments.

### Famous city

**Bellmare** — a massive mixed-species port built around seven connected harbors.

It is famous for:
- universities;
- shipbuilders;
- restaurants;
- theaters;
- newspapers;
- political demonstrations;
- ridiculous street festivals.

### Internal conflict

The wealthy coastal cities increasingly dominate the poorer inland districts.

The central government says infrastructure investment will fix the imbalance.

Inland leaders argue that the system itself favors the coast.

### Adventure potential

Gopal can get involved in:
- a city election;
- a shipbuilding competition;
- a university festival;
- a workers' strike;
- a criminal investigation;
- a ridiculous local sports tournament.

None requires the legendary destination.

# 3. THE VARESH EMPIRE — THE OLD GIANT

### Region
Central Continent.

### Political structure

A hereditary imperial system controlling an enormous territory through:
- provincial governors;
- military roads;
- taxation;
- regional courts;
- local rulers.

The Empire is old enough that several generations have never known another government.

### Culture

Varesh civilization values:
- discipline;
- education;
- military service;
- architecture;
- formal etiquette;
- historical continuity.

But ordinary people are not constantly serious.

Varesh cities have:
- food markets;
- comedians;
- children's games;
- taverns;
- theaters;
- festivals;
- street vendors.

### Architecture

Huge roads and bridges cross the Empire.

Ancient engineering is still used because replacing it would be absurdly expensive.

Some villages literally grew around bridges built 600 years ago.

### Internal conflict

The Empire is currently reforming.

Young officials want greater local autonomy.

Traditionalists fear fragmentation.

Frontier provinces increasingly resent taxes.

The imperial military is powerful but overstretched.

### Adventure potential

- imperial road journeys;
- border conflicts;
- royal tournaments;
- outlaw territories;
- provincial rebellions;
- ancient engineering failures;
- political marriages;
- Gopal accidentally becoming involved in a succession dispute.

# 4. THE TALASSI DELTA LEAGUE — CITIES OF THE WATER

### Region
Southern Sea / enormous river delta.

### Political structure

Not one kingdom.

It is a network of semi-independent river cities that cooperate on:
- water management;
- flood control;
- shipping;
- fishing rights;
- disease prevention;
- navigation.

### Population

Talassi are numerous, but humans, Varkai, Saaru and other peoples also live there.

### Civilization

The League's greatest achievement is its water infrastructure.

Canals, reservoirs, floating farms and flood gates allow millions to live in areas that would otherwise flood.

### Culture

The Delta is famous for:
- night markets;
- river festivals;
- enormous food culture;
- boat racing;
- music;
- floating theaters.

### Major festival

**The Thousand Lantern Tide**

Once each year, thousands of floating lanterns are released into the waterways.

Every neighborhood has its own design.

### Internal conflict

The League is becoming industrialized.

Factories create wealth but pollute waterways.

Older fishers demand restrictions.

Industrial cities demand jobs.

No side is entirely wrong.

### Adventure potential

- river races;
- floods;
- canal chases;
- factory sabotage;
- family businesses;
- fishing expeditions;
- political disputes;
- rescue missions.

# 5. THE KORRUN HOLDS — THE MOUNTAIN CONFEDERACY

### Region
Northern interior / major mountain ranges.

### Political structure

A loose confederation of mountain territories.

Each Hold controls its own valleys and towns.

They unite mainly for:
- defense;
- mountain roads;
- mining agreements;
- disaster response.

### Culture

The Holds have a strong tradition of communal craftsmanship.

A village repairing a bridge can become a festival.

A child may know basic mechanics before formal schooling.

### Famous sport

**Cliffball**

Teams race across enormous vertical arenas using ropes, platforms and controlled gliding equipment.

It is spectacular and slightly insane.

### Economy

Major exports:
- metals;
- stone;
- machinery;
- precision tools;
- cold-weather goods.

### Internal conflict

Mining companies are becoming increasingly powerful.

Some Hold leaders welcome industrial investment.

Others fear the mountains are being permanently damaged.

### Adventure potential

- mountain races;
- mines;
- avalanches;
- industrial espionage;
- engineering contests;
- climbing;
- lost caravans;
- festivals.

---

# 6. THE NOCTIS CITIES — THE NIGHT BELT

### Region
Southern and eastern desert-edge territories.

### Political structure

A chain of independent cities connected by caravan roads.

There is no single government.

Trade agreements keep the region functioning.

### Culture

The cities truly come alive after sunset.

Markets open at dusk.

Schools and workshops can operate at night.

Major celebrations happen under the stars.

### Architecture

Buildings use:
- shaded courtyards;
- underground rooms;
- reflective stone;
- cooling towers;
- water channels;
- colored glass.

### Famous city

**Saharim** — a huge inland market city where dozens of languages are spoken.

### Economy

Famous for:
- spices;
- glass;
- textiles;
- astronomy;
- caravan trade;
- medicinal plants.

### Internal conflict

Water rights are becoming the region's most important political issue.

Some cities control ancient wells.

Others depend on imported water.

### Adventure potential

- caravan journeys;
- desert races;
- water disputes;
- night festivals;
- thieves;
- archaeological expeditions;
- dangerous storms.

---

# 7. THE AERIE STATES — THE CLIFF REPUBLICS

### Region
Broken Continent.

### Political structure

Independent city-states built across enormous cliffs and plateaus.

They cooperate through a shared aerial transport network.

### Culture

The Aerie States are famous for:
- gliding;
- architecture;
- rescue services;
- aerial sports;
- wind engineering.

### Major city

**Avarra**

A city built across three enormous cliffs connected by bridges, cable systems and landing platforms.

### Social structure

Old families control some major cliff districts.

New industrialists control the growing lower cities.

Young people increasingly move downward because the old districts are expensive.

### Internal conflict

The central issue is not race.

It is access.

Who owns the sky routes?

Who controls the lifts?

Who can afford to live near the upper platforms?

### Adventure potential

- aerial races;
- cliff rescues;
- industrial sabotage;
- bridge disasters;
- political demonstrations;
- glider competitions.

---

# 8. THE MORUAN LONG VALLEYS — THE GENERATION LANDS

### Region
Eastern Sea interior / fertile river valleys.

### Political structure

A collection of old agricultural republics, family estates and town councils.

### Culture

Moru communities often contain many generations living simultaneously.

Old family houses can contain rooms built by ancestors centuries ago.

### Economy

Famous for:
- agriculture;
- medicine;
- wine/fruit cultivation;
- paper;
- education;
- traditional crafts.

### Cultural conflict

The older generation owns much of the land.

Younger Moru want:
- land reform;
- new industries;
- urban opportunities;
- less family control.

Human and other-species migrants increasingly settle in the cities.

### Major festival

**The First Harvest**

Every household prepares food representing a different generation of the family.

Some families make 200-year-old recipes.

Others deliberately cook something completely new.

### Adventure potential

- family disputes;
- festivals;
- agricultural disasters;
- inheritance conflicts;
- old estates;
- school adventures;
- political reform.

---

# 9. THE NEMATIC FREE CITIES — THE ART COAST

### Region
Opener Sea.

### Political structure

A collection of independent commercial cities.

No king.

No empire.

Each city survives through trade and specialized industries.

### Culture

The Free Cities are famous for:
- fashion;
- theater;
- painting;
- sculpture;
- music;
- architecture;
- publishing;
- performance.

### Famous city

**Mira Vey**

A city where public walls are legally available for art.

Entire neighborhoods change appearance every year.

### Economy

The region has enormous cultural industries.

A singer can become internationally famous.

A restaurant can become a destination.

A designer can influence fashion across several seas.

### Internal conflict

The artistic economy creates inequality.

Some artists become wealthy celebrities.

Others cannot afford housing.

Corporations increasingly buy public cultural spaces.

### Adventure potential

- music competitions;
- celebrity scandals;
- art theft;
- underground theater;
- fashion wars;
- street festivals;
- criminal syndicates.

# 10. THE WILDERMARCH — THE FRONTIER

### Region
Uncharted interior between major civilizations.

### Political structure

No unified government.

Instead:
- frontier towns;
- independent settlements;
- mining camps;
- farming communities;
- exploration companies;
- local militias;
- criminal groups.

World Patrol presence is limited.

### Culture

People here tend to be practical.

A famous explorer may be less respected than the person who keeps the town's water pump working.

### Major settlement

**Dustbell**

A rough but surprisingly friendly frontier city.

It has:
- one enormous train station;
- six competing inns;
- three newspapers;
- a terrible mayor;
- excellent food;
- a notorious bounty office.

### Internal conflict

Corporations are buying frontier land.

Settlers want stability.

Indigenous/local communities resist displacement.

Explorers want access to new territory.

Criminal groups exploit the confusion.

### Adventure potential

Almost unlimited:
- treasure hunting;
- exploration;
- local villains;
- bounty hunting;
- train journeys;
- frontier wars;
- wildlife;
- lost towns;
- ordinary friendships.

This region can become one of Gopal's earliest major adventure environments.

# 11. THE FREE HARBOR OF CALDER — NO COUNTRY'S CITY

### Region
Opener Sea.

### Political structure

Technically independent.

Practically controlled by competing:
- merchant houses;
- dock unions;
- criminal organizations;
- ship captains;
- local councils.

World Patrol has a permanent office but does not fully control the city.

### Culture

Calder is where the world collides.

You can find:
- every major language;
- foods from every sea;
- bounty hunters;
- adventurers;
- refugees;
- performers;
- smugglers;
- diplomats;
- pirates;
- merchants.

### Famous tradition

Every evening, one enormous public clock rings.

When it rings, the harbor restaurants compete to feed as many people as possible for one hour.

The tradition began during an old famine.

Nobody remembers exactly who started it.

Everyone continues it.

### Adventure potential

This can become a recurring location.

Characters can return years later and find:
- businesses changed;
- old friends older;
- new gangs;
- new laws;
- different ships;
- different neighborhoods.

Calder should feel like a living crossroads rather than a one-arc set.

# 12. THE WORLD PATROL RELATIONSHIP

The World Patrol does not rule these civilizations.

It interacts with them differently.

### Commonwealth
Strong legal cooperation.

### Varesh Empire
Formal cooperation with frequent political tension.

### Delta League
Essential disaster-response partnership.

### Korrun Holds
Strong mountain rescue relationship.

### Noctis Cities
Limited patrol presence; local authorities retain most law enforcement.

### Aerie States
Joint aerial rescue agreements.

### Moruan Valleys
Mostly diplomatic and trade-focused.

### Nem Free Cities
Heavy anti-smuggling operations, but complicated by local independence.

### Wildermarch
Under-patrolled and difficult to govern.

### Calder
Permanent patrol presence but constant jurisdictional conflict.

This means the World Patrol does not become a simple "good government" or "evil government."

Its role depends on location.

# 13. CIVILIZATION SCALE

These nine zones are NOT the whole world.

Each contains:
- smaller countries;
- villages;
- islands;
- city-states;
- ethnic regions;
- autonomous territories;
- religious communities;
- colonies/former colonies if the historical design supports them;
- disputed borders;
- independent settlements.

The reader should gradually discover the world.

Do not dump the entire map in Chapter 1.

# 14. THE WORLD IS CURRENTLY CHANGING

The current era should not be politically frozen.

Several changes are happening simultaneously:

### Industrial expansion
Factories and new transport systems are spreading.

### Increased long-distance travel
More ordinary people can travel between regions.

### Information acceleration
New communication technology is making news travel faster.

### World Patrol expansion
The organization is increasing its presence in frontier regions.

### Bounty culture
Adventurers and bounty hunters are becoming more visible.

### Species migration
Mixed-species cities are growing.

### Old powers weakening
Some old empires and aristocratic institutions are losing influence.

### New powers rising
Merchant corporations, independent cities and private organizations are becoming influential.

### Exploration boom
The public obsession with the legendary destination is creating:
- expeditions;
- scams;
- treasure hunters;
- scholars;
- adventurers;
- cults;
- tourism;
- businesses.

But most ordinary people still have ordinary lives.

---

# 15. IMPORTANT: THE DESTINATION IS NOT EVERYONE'S LIFE

A baker in a small village may know the legendary destination exists.

They may have:
- never met an adventurer;
- never traveled by ship;
- never cared about reaching it.

A teenager might dream of becoming a singer.

A farmer might worry about next year's harvest.

A child might want to win a local race.

A mechanic might want to build a better engine.

A family might simply want to keep their restaurant open.

This is essential.

The legendary destination is the world's great horizon.

It is not the only reason people wake up in the morning.

# 16. Gopal'S FUTURE EXPERIENCE

As Gopal travels, he should not think:

> "How does this help me reach the legendary place?"

He should often think:

> "That looks fun."

or:

> "Those people need help."

or:

> "I've never seen anything like that."

or:

> "I want to try that."

or:

> "Why are these idiots fighting over a cooking contest?"

Sometimes the crew will deliberately take a detour.

Sometimes the detour will become one of the most important adventures of their lives.

Sometimes they will regret it.

Sometimes they will remember it decades later.

That is the adventure.

# 18. WHAT WE HAVE NOW

We now have three world layers:

### Layer 1 — Geography
Seas, continents, travel systems, climate and natural environments.

### Layer 2 — Peoples
Different biological peoples with internal cultural diversity.

### Layer 3 — Civilizations
Political societies created by history, geography, migration, economy and belief.

Next comes:

### Layer 4 — Organizations
World Patrol, bounty institutions, exploration companies, merchant powers, criminal networks, religious organizations, scientific institutions and other groups.

Then:

### Layer 5 — Everyday economy
Money, trade, jobs, ships, food supply, newspapers, communication, education and ordinary life.

Then:

### Layer 6 — Power system
Only after the world itself is grounded.

Then we can finally build:

**Gopal's home → childhood environment → first major companion → first saga.**

---

# 19. DESIGN PRINCIPLE

> **A civilization should be capable of producing ten different stories before Gopal ever arrives.**

If it cannot, the civilization is decoration rather than worldbuilding.

# STATUS

All civilization names, governments and details are **PROVISIONAL**.

The permanent direction is:

> The world contains many civilizations with overlapping peoples, competing interests, ordinary lives and independent histories. Gopal travels through this world rather than traveling through a sequence of mystery boxes.

The legendary destination remains the distant horizon.

The adventure remains the story.
