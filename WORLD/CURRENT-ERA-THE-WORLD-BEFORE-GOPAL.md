# CURRENT ERA — THE WORLD BEFORE GOPAL

> Status: PROVISIONAL
> Purpose: establish a living present-day world before Gopal becomes important.

## Core principle

**The world was already moving. Then Gopal entered it.**

The current era is not a prologue waiting for Gopal. Wars, discoveries, careers, scandals, festivals, crimes, inventions, disasters, romances, elections, expeditions and ordinary lives are already happening.

Most of them are not secretly about Gopal.

## 1. The Age of Open Horizons

The world is entering a period of unusually rapid movement.

Long-distance shipping is becoming cheaper. New routes are being charted. Newspapers and messenger networks spread news faster than previous generations experienced. Industrial centers are growing. World Patrol offices are expanding. Independent adventurers are becoming celebrities. The bounty economy is becoming entertainment as well as law enforcement.

At the same time, old political structures are under pressure.

The result is a restless generation:

- young people want to travel;
- governments want to control routes;
- merchants want new markets;
- scientists want unexplored environments;
- criminals want new territory;
- families fear what rapid change will cost them;
- ordinary workers want better lives;
- adventurers want to see what lies beyond the known map.

The legendary destination is one major force inside this age, but it is not the cause of every change.

## 2. Famous People of the Current Era

Famous people should exist for different reasons. Being famous does not mean being powerful.

### World Patrol

**Marshal Iven Rook — PROVISIONAL**

A senior World Patrol commander known for ending a coastal war without destroying either capital. He is respected by many officers and criticized by some politicians for refusing to treat military victory as the only measure of success.

**Captain Sera Vale — PROVISIONAL**

A patrol captain famous for disaster rescues rather than combat. Her crew specializes in storms, collapsing ports and evacuation missions. Children know her name from illustrated rescue stories.

### Explorers

**Mira Sol — PROVISIONAL**

A young cartographer whose expedition redrew a dangerous section of the southern ocean. She is famous because she published the route openly instead of selling it exclusively.

**Daro Venn — PROVISIONAL**

An older explorer who has spent forty years traveling. He is famous for refusing to exaggerate his discoveries. His maps contain large areas marked simply: “I don't know.”

### Bounty hunters

**Kesh Ardan — PROVISIONAL**

A Red Mile hunter known for capturing dangerous criminals alive. His unusual reputation comes from refusing bounties that would endanger uninvolved civilians.

**The Bell Sisters — PROVISIONAL**

Two unrelated bounty hunters who operate as partners and have become popular performers in public demonstrations. They are competent but constantly argue about whose idea saved the mission.

### Criminals

**Vargo Pell — PROVISIONAL**

A port-city smuggler whose organization specializes in moving medicine, people and contraband through blockades. He is neither romanticized nor treated as a cartoon villain; some communities owe him their survival while authorities consider him a major criminal.

**Mother Coil — PROVISIONAL**

An Ashen Hand broker who rarely commits violence personally. She buys information, identities, routes and favors. Her power comes from knowing who needs what.

### Science and invention

**Professor Talen Or — PROVISIONAL**

A Circle of Living Science researcher studying dangerous environmental anomalies. He is famous for publicly admitting when an experiment fails.

**Nemi Quill — PROVISIONAL**

An inventor whose cheap navigation instruments became common among ordinary sailors. Her work is changing travel without making her a fighter.

### Culture and sport

**Jaro Bell — PROVISIONAL**

A champion of the international sport of Cliffball. His fame reaches people who could not care less about explorers or bounty hunters.

**Luma Rei — PROVISIONAL**

A traveling musician whose songs about ports and ordinary workers become unexpectedly famous across species and regions.

## 3. Current Conflicts

### The Varesh Border Crisis

A disputed frontier between the Varesh Empire and neighboring autonomous communities has become increasingly militarized.

The dispute concerns land, trade routes, old treaties and local identity. Neither side is uniformly correct or uniformly corrupt.

World Patrol observers are present, but their involvement is politically sensitive.

### The Talassi Water Dispute

Industrial projects upstream from the Talassi Delta League are changing seasonal water flow.

Engineers argue that the projects prevent floods and create energy. Downstream communities argue that they threaten farms, fisheries and traditional waterways.

This conflict can produce protests, negotiations, sabotage, scientific investigations and ordinary family consequences without becoming a world-ending war.

### Wildermarch Land Rush

Companies and private investors are buying frontier land rapidly.

Some settlements welcome jobs and roads. Others fear losing land, access to water or control of their communities.

Criminal groups exploit the confusion.

### Noctis Water Rights

Several desert cities are renegotiating ancient water agreements because population and industry are increasing.

This creates political conflict, trade pressure, migration and occasional violence.

## 4. Current Disasters and Recent Events

### The Glasswind Storm — PROVISIONAL

A strange but natural atmospheric event recently devastated several coastal settlements.

The disaster became famous because people from rival countries cooperated during rescue operations.

The World Patrol gained public support in some regions and criticism in others because aid distribution was uneven.

### The Calder Dock Fire — PROVISIONAL

A huge international port fire destroyed warehouses, ships and homes.

The cause remains disputed between accident, criminal sabotage and negligence.

The important consequence is not the mystery itself: insurance disputes, displaced workers, reconstruction contracts, criminal investigations and political arguments continue years later.

### The Broken Road Floods — PROVISIONAL

Unusually severe flooding damaged inland transportation routes, temporarily isolating communities.

Travelers, traders and local volunteers became more famous than many official leaders because they kept food moving.

## 5. The Exploration Race

Exploration has become fashionable.

There are:

- independent crews;
- scientific expeditions;
- wealthy private expeditions;
- government missions;
- religious pilgrimages;
- bounty hunters searching dangerous ruins;
- treasure seekers;
- fraudsters selling fake maps;
- ordinary tourists following famous routes.

Several groups are searching for Passage Keys, but almost none understand the complete access system.

Some genuine Keys are known to exist.

Some are lost.

Some are disputed.

Some objects sold as Keys are fake.

This creates an entire economy around authentication, theft, scholarship and fraud.

The existence of Keys is therefore a current-world fact before Gopal understands their importance.

## 6. The Bounty Culture

Bounties are administrative tools, not a perfect ranking of combat strength.

A person's bounty can reflect:

- crimes;
- threat to civilians;
- damage caused;
- ability to evade authorities;
- political importance;
- information value;
- repeated offenses;
- interference with major institutions.

A famous fighter can have a modest bounty.

A noncombatant information broker can have a surprisingly high one.

This keeps the bounty system useful without turning it into a simple power ladder.

## 7. World Seed Users in the Current Era

World Seeds are rare enough that most people never meet a user personally.

But Seed users are now increasingly visible because exploration, black markets, scientific research and organized crime are expanding.

Some famous Seed users are:

- rescue workers;
- criminals;
- athletes;
- doctors;
- ship engineers;
- soldiers;
- performers;
- bounty hunters;
- farmers;
- explorers.

### HARD POWER LAW — NO DUPLICATE ASPECTS

**No two people may possess the same World Seed Aspect.**

Once an Aspect is assigned to a character, that exact supernatural rule is retired from future character creation.

Different abilities may interact with the same physical phenomenon, but they must operate through different underlying rules.

Example:

- Friction
- Momentum
- Adhesion
- Surface Texture

can all affect movement while remaining four distinct Aspects.

We must maintain a **Power Registry** so future characters cannot accidentally duplicate an existing power.

Every significant Seed user receives:

**Aspect → Gift → Tithe → Limits → Counters → Applications → User history**

The system must also avoid giving every major character a Seed. Extraordinary fighters can come from training, technology, tactics, medicine, weapons, natural ability and eventually Spiritual Power.

## 8. Things Happening While Gopal Is Still a Child

These events exist independently of him:

- a young explorer completes a famous voyage;
- a World Patrol officer becomes a public hero after a disaster;
- a criminal syndicate loses an important port;
- a new industrial route opens;
- a famous athlete retires;
- a scientist publishes a controversial discovery;
- a Passage Key is stolen from a private collection;
- a festival becomes internationally famous;
- a local war ends badly but does not make global headlines;
- a child in another country meets the person who will later become important to Gopal;
- Ren's life changes after the childhood incident;
- a future major antagonist begins making decisions somewhere Gopal has never heard of.

Most of these events should not be explained to the reader immediately.

The world can introduce them later through newspapers, rumors, returning characters, changed cities, scars, new laws, old friendships and consequences.

## 9. The World Does Not Wait

If Gopal spends twenty chapters somewhere, something elsewhere may change.

A port can fall under new leadership.

A criminal can escape.

A famous athlete can retire.

A war can end.

A friend can get married.

A company can become powerful.

A town can rebuild after disaster.

A World Patrol officer can be promoted.

A Passage Key can change hands.

A character Gopal met fifty chapters ago can have an entirely different life when they return.

This is one of the central realism rules of the manga.

## 10. Current Era Tone

The era should feel exciting rather than apocalyptic.

There is danger, but there is also:

- music;
- food;
- sports;
- festivals;
- school;
- romance;
- stupid arguments;
- ambitious teenagers;
- tired workers;
- families;
- tourists;
- comedians;
- inventors;
- fishermen;
- teachers;
- criminals who are idiots;
- criminals who are terrifying;
- heroes nobody writes songs about.

The reader should feel that if Gopal disappeared tomorrow, the planet would still have stories.

## 11. Gopal's Relationship to the Current Era

Gopal is not introduced as the center of this historical moment.

At the beginning, famous people do not automatically know his name.

Major organizations do not automatically care about him.

The legendary destination is already being pursued before he enters the race.

His importance grows because of choices, relationships, consequences and the strange path his life takes.

He earns attention rather than receiving it from the premise.

## 12. Ren's Parallel Beginning

While Gopal grows up in his own environment, Ren's life continues elsewhere.

Ren is not waiting in a prison until the plot needs him.

He is learning, surviving, meeting people, making mistakes, building trust, losing trust, discovering parts of the world, and eventually creating his own group.

His path intersects with the current world's organizations and conflicts independently.

By the time Gopal eventually hears his name again, Ren should already be a person with history.

## 13. Famous Does Not Mean Future Main Character

Some current-era characters will remain famous background figures.

Some will become recurring allies.

Some will become enemies.

Some will disappear from the story after one historical event.

Some will die off-screen before Gopal ever meets them.

Some will never meet Gopal at all.

This prevents the world from feeling like every important person was secretly created for his story.

## 14. Current Era Design Rule

Whenever we create a major current-era event, ask:

1. Who benefits?
2. Who loses?
3. What changes afterward?
4. What ordinary people experience?
5. Which institution reacts?
6. What misinformation spreads?
7. What becomes common knowledge?
8. What remains unknown?
9. Does this event matter even without Gopal?
10. Can its consequences return much later?

## 15. Next Dependency

The current era now exists as a living baseline.

Next we move inward:

**GOPAL'S HOME REGION → GOPAL'S FAMILY / CHILDHOOD LIFE → THE CHILDHOOD INCIDENT → FIRST SAGA ARCHITECTURE.**

The first saga will then be designed around Gopal entering an already-moving world rather than simply leaving home and meeting a new person every chapter.
