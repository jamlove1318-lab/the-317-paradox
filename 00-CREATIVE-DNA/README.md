# Phase 0 — Creative DNA

**Status:** IN PROGRESS

## Working Method
- **CANON** = explicitly approved direction; safe to build on.
- **PROVISIONAL** = under discussion; not canon.
- **REJECTED** = explicitly ruled out.
- **OPEN** = still needs exploration.

Conversation brainstorming does not automatically become canon. A decision becomes canon only after Gopal approves it.

## CANON — Current Decisions

### 1. Feeling Over Features
The manga should create genuine emotion rather than collect popular ingredients. Characters, places, relationships, mysteries and conflicts should earn love, excitement, sadness, happiness, curiosity, fear, wonder, laughter, shock and attachment. No tragedy merely for shock, twist merely for a twist, or spectacle merely because it looks cool.

### 2. Adventure Is Unpredictable
Adventure may become discovery, wonder, comedy, mystery, attachment, danger, beauty, horror, heartbreak or something unexpected. Place, characters and circumstances determine the experience rather than a fixed formula.

### 3. World Beyond the Protagonist
The world must feel larger than Gopal and continue living beyond his immediate story.

### 4. A Living World
Places are homes, cultures, ecosystems, histories and memories—not just stages for Gopal. Meaningful places can have people whose lives are unrelated to him, local customs, food, language, art, beliefs, humor, routines, disagreements, local stories and consequences that remain after he leaves. Readers should sometimes care about a place before knowing why it matters.

### 5. The Horizon Is Always Larger
There are always meaningful things beyond current knowledge: known and unknown lands, distant cultures, difficult-to-reach places, visible borders hiding complexity, and discoveries that expand the perceived scale of the world. Answers may create larger questions, but mystery must not become empty withholding.

### 6. Worlds Within Worlds
A location can gain new meaning as knowledge changes. Traditions, monuments, routes and legends can preserve distorted fragments of history. Revelations should ideally deepen earlier worldbuilding rather than erase it.

### 7. The World Has Memory
History survives through ruins, architecture, roads, songs, names, languages, family traditions, festivals, heirlooms, landscapes, technology, customs, memorials, missing places and things people refuse to discuss. History should be encountered, not only explained.

### 8. Many Ways of Seeing
Children, farmers, rulers, explorers, scientists, priests, criminals, historians, soldiers, artists and isolated communities can interpret the same reality differently. Perspectives may conflict without one side being completely foolish or completely correct.

### 9. Wonder and Absurdity Coexist
The world can contain bizarre customs, ridiculous laws, strange foods, eccentric architecture, unusual occupations and impossible-looking phenomena. A culture can be hilarious in one moment and heartbreaking in another.

### 10. Some Things Feel Incomprehensible
Some phenomena may eventually be explained, partially understood, or remain fundamentally strange. The unknown should create awe, curiosity, unease or humility—not random confusion.

### 11. Places Readers Can Miss
Places should have enough identity to create attachment. On later visits, people, relationships, buildings, traditions, problems, Gopal and the reader's understanding may all have changed.

### 12. The World Moves Without Gopal
Events continue elsewhere: negotiations, relationships, businesses, discoveries, conflicts, festivals, mistakes and dreams. Not everything must intersect with Gopal.

### 13. The Reader Can Know Before Gopal
The story can deliberately create different knowledge levels between reader and characters. Sometimes the reader sees a clue first; sometimes another character knows more; sometimes Gopal notices what the reader missed.

### 14. The World Changes
Across a long story, people, relationships, cities, borders, technology, trade, traditions, reputations, environments, myths and political structures can change. Time should feel real.

### 15. The World Is Not Only Plot
Not every detail must be a clue, secret, power explanation or future twist. Some things exist simply because people naturally created them. A bakery can just be a bakery; a side character can have a small life that never becomes central.

### 16. Controlled Freedom
The master plan protects deep mysteries and long-term architecture without explaining every small detail in advance. New ideas may emerge during writing if they respect established canon and strengthen the story.
