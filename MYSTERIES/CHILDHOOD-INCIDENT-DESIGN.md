# CHILDHOOD INCIDENT — DESIGN FOUNDATION

> Status: PROVISIONAL
> This is the next major story foundation after Ren's character design.
> Exact names, terminology and final mechanics remain open.

---

## 1. CORE IDEA

Young Gopal and Ren do not discover a treasure, ancient weapon, prophecy, or complete map.

They discover something much stranger:

> **A place that exists where no place should be able to exist.**

More specifically, they encounter evidence that a location can appear in the world without having a continuous physical route to it.

The evidence is small enough for children to encounter accidentally.

The implications are enormous.

This gives the legendary destination a unique structural identity without simply making it "the place with the biggest treasure."

---

## 2. THE CHILDREN'S DISCOVERY

Gopal and Ren live in an ordinary region.

The place has an old local story about a landmark that disappeared generations ago.

Adults treat the story as folklore.

Children use it for games.

Merchants tell exaggerated versions to travelers.

Older people disagree about whether it was ever real.

One day, Gopal and Ren encounter a physical object/phenomenon connected to the missing landmark.

It appears to show a location that should be somewhere else.

The exact visual form is TBD.

The important feature:

> **The evidence behaves as though distance does not apply to the legendary place.**

The boys cannot explain it.

They begin investigating.

---

## 3. WHY IT MATTERS EMOTIONALLY

The discovery should not begin as a giant lore mystery.

The boys initially care because it is exciting.

They want to know whether the old story is true.

They argue over it.

They make childish plans.

They imagine what they would do if they reached the place.

They make fun of adults who say it is impossible.

This keeps the beginning human.

Only later does the reader discover that their harmless curiosity crossed into something dangerous.

---

## 4. WHAT THEY ACTUALLY SEE

PROVISIONAL VISUAL CONCEPT:

They find an old object that should contain a normal image/map/record.

Instead, at a certain natural condition, it shows a distant location that cannot be physically reached from their current geography.

The image is not a clean map.

It is incomplete.

It changes.

There is something in the background that matches an old symbol from the legendary stories.

Ren notices a detail.

Gopal notices something else.

Neither understands the complete picture.

This allows their memories of the incident to differ.

---

## 5. THE IMPORTANT DETAIL

Ren notices that the impossible location is not simply somewhere far away.

Something about it suggests:

> **The place is capable of being somewhere different depending on circumstances.**

This becomes an early seed for the later revelation that the legendary destination is not simply a fixed coordinate.

The final mechanism is NOT decided.

---

## 6. WHY REN REMEMBERS MORE

Ren is naturally observant.

During the discovery he notices:

- a symbol;
- a sequence;
- a sound;
- a person's reaction;
- and one physical detail Gopal overlooks.

Gopal remembers the emotional event more strongly.

Ren remembers the evidence more strongly.

This creates complementary memories.

Neither child possesses the full truth.

---

## 7. THE ADULT WHO NOTICES

Someone outside the boys' world realizes they found something.

This person should not be the ultimate antagonist.

They are an intermediary.

They recognize the significance of the evidence.

They become frightened.

Their reaction is the first clue that this is bigger than childhood folklore.

They report the discovery to a larger organization.

The exact organization is TBD.

---

## 8. WHY THEY TAKE REN

The abductors do not know that Gopal and Ren are equally important.

Ren is the easier target because:

- he remembers more;
- he asked more questions;
- he may have kept notes;
- he returned to investigate;
- or someone overheard him describing the discovery.

Gopal is not chosen because there is no evidence he understands enough to be useful.

This is circumstantial, not destiny.

---

## 9. THE ABDUCTION

The event should happen while the boys are still ordinary.

There is no huge supernatural battle.

No child transformation.

No secret bloodline reveal.

No convenient adult hero.

The danger is frightening precisely because the boys are powerless.

Ren realizes something is wrong.

He tries to protect the evidence.

Gopal tries to interfere.

They are separated.

Ren is taken.

Gopal survives.

---

## 10. WHAT GOPAL THINKS HAPPENED

For years, Gopal believes Ren disappeared because of the legendary place.

He does not know whether Ren is dead.

He carries guilt.

He also carries the promise.

But Gopal's understanding is incomplete.

He does not know that Ren survived.

He does not know who took him.

He does not know that Ren's missing years changed the shape of the original mystery.

---

## 11. WHAT REN THINKS HAPPENED

Ren initially believes Gopal may also have been taken.

He spends part of his captivity trying to learn whether Gopal survived.

Eventually he discovers that Gopal was left behind.

This creates complicated feelings:

- relief;
- anger;
- loneliness;
- confusion;
- eventually acceptance.

Ren does not blame Gopal for surviving.

But the experience permanently changes how Ren thinks about abandonment and responsibility.

---

## 12. THE FALSE ASSUMPTION

The childhood evidence does NOT directly prove:

"The legendary place exists."

Instead it proves something stranger.

It proves that the old stories contain a factual element that modern history cannot explain.

This distinction is important.

Gopal remembers:

"Ren believed the place was real."

Ren remembers:

"We saw something that made the old story impossible to dismiss."

Those are not exactly the same thing.

---

## 13. LONG-TERM MYSTERY STRUCTURE

The reader should eventually encounter several versions of the childhood incident.

### Version 1 — Gopal's memory
Two kids discovered something impossible and Ren disappeared.

### Version 2 — Ren's memory
They discovered something more specific than Gopal remembers.

### Version 3 — The intermediary's record
The discovery triggered a response from people who already knew the phenomenon existed.

### Version 4 — Historical record
The same phenomenon may have happened before.

### Version 5 — Legendary event connection
The phenomenon is related to the historical event that created the worldwide pursuit.

### Version 6 — Ultimate antagonist connection
The people controlling the information have been protecting or exploiting it for generations.

### Version 7 — Final truth
The phenomenon is a natural/structural property of the legendary destination and explains why the destination cannot be understood as a normal place.

Each version should add information rather than simply contradict the previous one.

---

## 14. THE BIG ORIGINALITY REQUIREMENT

The destination must NOT become:

- a hidden island copied from another series;
- a treasure room;
- a giant weapon;
- a simple alternate dimension;
- a generic "void";
- a time machine;
- a secret heaven;
- a final dungeon.

The strange property must become something original to this world.

The current candidate is:

> **A place whose relationship to geography is fundamentally different from ordinary places.**

The final explanation is intentionally open.

---

## 15. WHY THIS CONNECTS TO REN

Ren's childhood experience explains his adult dream.

He learned that people with power can make inconvenient discoveries disappear.

He therefore becomes obsessed with protecting people and information that powerful institutions want erased.

This connects naturally to his independent organization.

He is not simply trying to reunite with Gopal.

He is trying to make sure what happened to them cannot happen to someone else.

---

## 16. WHY THIS CONNECTS TO GOPAL

Gopal carries the emotional side.

He remembers:

- the promise;
- Ren disappearing;
- the feeling that something impossible was real;
- the belief that one day he would show Ren.

He does not initially understand the larger system.

His journey gradually teaches him.

This allows the audience to learn about the world alongside him without making Gopal ignorant for artificial reasons.

---

## 17. WHY THIS CONNECTS TO THE ULTIMATE ANTAGONIST

The ultimate antagonist's organization should have a historical relationship to controlling information about the phenomenon.

The antagonist does not need to have personally caused the childhood abduction.

Instead, the organization can have inherited a system.

This is more interesting than:

"Villain kidnapped Ren because he knew the secret."

The villain's ideology can eventually involve the belief that some truths are too dangerous to be freely accessible.

This gives Ren and the antagonist an ideological connection.

---

## 18. THE CHILDHOOD SCENE WE WANT

Eventually, in a flashback hundreds of chapters later, readers see the entire childhood sequence.

At first it looked like:

Two kids joking around.

Then:

They find something.

Then:

They realize something is wrong.

Then:

They make the promise.

Then:

Someone arrives.

Then:

Ren is taken.

The flashback ends before revealing everything.

Later another flashback completes it.

The emotional power comes from seeing an ordinary childhood memory become the origin point of the entire story.

---

## 19. IMPORTANT: DO NOT MAKE THIS THE ONLY REASON GOPAL TRAVELS

Gopal must eventually develop relationships, interests, dreams and experiences that exist independently of Ren.

He does not spend hundreds of chapters emotionally frozen.

He grows.

He laughs.

He makes friends.

He falls into stupid situations.

He learns.

He fails.

He becomes stronger.

He develops his own dream.

Ren remains important, but does not consume Gopal's life.

---

## 20. THE SAME IS TRUE FOR REN

Ren does not spend his missing years thinking:

"Where is Gopal?"

His life continues.

He meets people.

He loses people.

He builds something.

He becomes responsible for others.

He makes mistakes.

He develops a dream.

He becomes strong.

He changes.

When Gopal eventually meets him, Ren should be someone the reader could already care about independently.

---

## 21. NEXT DEPENDENCY

This incident now gives us the structural bridge to design:

### THE LEGENDARY EVENT

We need to answer:

- What happened historically?
- Why did the world remember it?
- Why does the destination have its impossible geographical property?
- Why did civilizations interpret it differently?
- How did it affect ordinary life?
- Why do millions of people care about the destination?
- Why do governments care?
- Why do criminals care?
- Why do scholars care?
- Why does the ultimate antagonist care?
- Why did Ren and Gopal accidentally touch its shadow as children?

The event should be spectacular enough to become legend, but consequential enough to shape civilization.

---
