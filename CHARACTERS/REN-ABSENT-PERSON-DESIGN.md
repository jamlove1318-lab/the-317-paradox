# ABSENT PERSON — REN (PROVISIONAL CHARACTER FOUNDATION)

> Status: PROVISIONAL
> This document is a foundation candidate, not final canon.
> Working name: Ren
> Core role: Gopal's childhood companion, separated by a world-level incident, later revealed to have built an independent life and power of his own.

---

## 1. CORE CONCEPT

Ren is not Gopal's lost sidekick.

He is the second protagonist of the hidden childhood story.

Gopal and Ren were ordinary children who discovered evidence that something the world considered impossible might actually exist. They became obsessed with reaching the legendary place associated with that impossibility.

They promised each other they would reach it together.

Then Ren disappeared.

Gopal eventually left home and continued toward the legendary place, believing that the promise might be all he had left of Ren.

But Ren was never dead.

He was abducted because of what the two boys discovered. He survived, escaped, and spent the missing years building an entirely separate life.

By the time Gopal learns Ren is alive, Ren is no longer the boy Gopal remembers.

He has:

- his own friends;
- his own enemies;
- his own reputation;
- his own organization;
- his own dream;
- his own philosophy;
- his own scars;
- his own strength;
- his own understanding of the legendary place.

He is somewhat less powerful than Gopal as an individual, but strong enough to defeat one of the ultimate antagonist's elite warriors without being rescued.

Most importantly:

Gopal and Ren do not need each other in order to be complete people.

Their reunion matters because they once shared something that neither of them ever truly abandoned.

---

# 2. CHILDHOOD REN

As a child, Ren is not the "smart one" in a simple sense.

He is observant, skeptical, stubborn and unusually good at noticing when adults are lying.

He likes rules when the rules make sense.

He hates rules that exist only because someone powerful says they exist.

He asks annoying questions.

Gopal acts.

Ren asks:

"Why?"

Gopal says:

"Because we can."

Ren says:

"That is not an answer."

Gopal:

"It worked."

Ren:

"That makes it worse."

Their friendship grows from this constant friction.

Gopal pulls Ren toward possibility.

Ren pulls Gopal toward consequence.

Neither is always right.

---

# 3. CHILDHOOD CONTRADICTION

Ren appears more responsible than Gopal.

But secretly, Ren is the more obsessive one.

Gopal can become interested in something and then get distracted by food, animals, a festival, a fight, a strange person, or something shiny.

Ren remembers.

If Ren sees an impossible mark carved into a wall, he will think about it for years.

If someone tells him a story that contradicts itself, he remembers the contradiction.

If a map has a blank space where there should be a city, he wants to know why.

This becomes both his strength and his weakness.

His greatest childhood flaw is:

> Ren has difficulty letting a question remain unanswered.

That flaw will later become dangerous.

---

# 4. WHAT Gopal AND REN DISCOVER

They do not discover the legendary place.

They discover a small piece of evidence that should not exist.

The exact object/evidence remains TBD.

The important properties are:

1. It is physical.
2. It cannot be explained by the ordinary history they have been taught.
3. It connects indirectly to the legendary place.
4. Someone powerful wants the evidence controlled or erased.
5. Gopal and Ren do not understand its significance.
6. Ren remembers details that Gopal forgets.
7. The evidence later becomes one of the keys connecting the childhood incident to the world's deeper history.

Their discovery should be something that, in isolation, looks almost harmless.

The horror is that someone cares enough to abduct a child over it.

---

# 5. THE PROMISE

After arguing about whether the legendary place is real, Ren says that one day they will reach it.

Gopal eventually makes the emotional promise:

"I'll show you that it really exists."

Ren answers:

"Then we'll see it together."

The wording is deliberately simple.

Neither child understands the weight of the promise.

That is important.

The meaning is created by the years that follow.

---

# 6. THE DISAPPEARANCE

Ren does not simply vanish during a random accident.

The childhood discovery attracts attention.

Adults begin asking questions.

Someone realizes Ren remembers something important.

Gopal is separated from Ren.

Ren is taken.

Gopal does not know whether Ren is alive.

The people responsible do not necessarily intend to kill him.

They want information.

They want to know what he saw, what he remembers, who else knows, and whether the children found something connected to the legendary place.

Ren refuses to cooperate.

His resistance is not because he is already heroic.

He is terrified.

He is a child.

He makes mistakes.

He gives away some information.

He lies badly.

He learns.

That experience is one of the things that eventually shapes the adult Ren.

---

# 7. WHY GOPAL SURVIVES

This must not be because Gopal was secretly chosen.

The separation should be circumstantial.

The antagonistic group believes Ren is the useful witness.

Gopal is not considered important.

This becomes an important part of Gopal's character.

He was not selected by destiny.

He was simply the other child who survived the event.

Later, this makes Gopal's growing importance more meaningful.

---

# 8. REN'S MISSING YEARS

Ren spends part of his adolescence moving through places and people he never would have encountered otherwise.

The exact chronology is TBD.

The important progression is:

CAPTURE
→ SURVIVAL
→ ESCAPE
→ DISBELIEF
→ ANGER
→ SEARCH FOR ANSWERS
→ MEETING PEOPLE
→ BUILDING TRUST
→ FAILURE
→ FIRST REAL CHOICE
→ FORMING HIS OWN GROUP
→ DEVELOPING HIS OWN DREAM
→ BECOMING A MAJOR INDEPENDENT FORCE

Ren eventually stops being defined by what happened to him.

This is crucial.

His life is not "the years Gopal was missing."

They are Ren's years.

---

# 9. REN'S ESCAPE

Ren eventually escapes through a combination of:

- observation;
- patience;
- another person's help;
- willingness to take a risk;
- understanding the environment.

He does not overpower his captors.

His first major victory is intellectual and human, not physical.

This establishes his later fighting style.

Ren learns early:

> You do not have to be stronger than the whole world to survive it.

You need to understand what is moving around you.

---

# 10. THE PERSON WHO HELPS REN

Ren should not escape completely alone.

A person helps him.

This person can later become one of the founding members of Ren's group.

Their relationship matters because it teaches Ren something Gopal never had to teach him:

Trust can be chosen after trust has been broken.

This person does not become Ren's replacement for Gopal.

They become their own character.

Their friendship is built in the missing years.

---

# 11. REN'S GROUP

Ren eventually forms a small independent organization.

Working name: THE WAYFARERS (PROVISIONAL).

This is not a pirate crew, military squad, or copy of Gopal's future group.

Their purpose initially grows from survival and information gathering.

Over time they become a real force.

Possible functions:

- exploration;
- intelligence;
- protection of isolated communities;
- recovery of dangerous artifacts;
- escorting people through unstable regions;
- investigating erased history;
- opposing groups that exploit the legendary-place pursuit.

They are not universally heroic.

They make mistakes.

They take jobs.

They have internal arguments.

They have debts.

They sometimes disagree with Ren.

Ren leads them because he earned their trust, not because the story declares him their captain.

---

# 12. REN'S LEADERSHIP STYLE

Gopal gathers people through emotional gravity.

Ren gathers people through trust and reliability.

Gopal can say:

"Let's go!"

and people sometimes follow because it sounds fun.

Ren says:

"I don't know if this will work. But if we do it, I won't leave anyone behind."

People follow because they believe him.

This distinction should remain throughout the story.

---

# 13. REN'S INDEPENDENT DREAM

Ren's dream should NOT be:

"Find Gopal."

It should NOT be:

"Reach the legendary place because Gopal promised."

And it should not simply be:

"Find the truth."

His dream grows out of his missing years.

PROVISIONAL CORE:

Ren wants to make sure that people who discover things powerful people want hidden cannot simply be erased.

In its simplest emotional form:

> Ren wants a world where discovering something dangerous does not mean disappearing.

This can later evolve into a larger goal involving knowledge, memory, and power.

His dream is therefore related to Gopal's story without being the same dream.

---

# 14. REN'S PHILOSOPHY

Ren believes:

> "Truth doesn't protect people. People protect truth."

Gopal initially disagrees.

Gopal believes people should be able to see the truth for themselves.

Ren argues that truth without preparation can destroy people.

Neither is completely right.

This becomes a recurring philosophical disagreement.

Their conflict is not:

"Good guy vs good guy."

It is:

"How much truth can people carry, and who gets to decide?"

---

# 15. REN'S BIGGEST FLAW

Ren can become controlling when he is afraid.

Because he once had no control over his own life, he becomes obsessed with controlling circumstances around the people he cares about.

He makes plans without telling everyone.

He hides information "for their safety."

He sometimes decides what other people need before asking them.

This creates conflict with Gopal.

Gopal's flaw is interfering impulsively.

Ren's flaw is controlling through preparation.

Both are forms of caring that can become harmful.

---

# 16. REN'S COMBAT IDENTITY

Ren should not fight like Gopal.

Gopal is instinctive, adaptive and increasingly unpredictable.

Ren is deliberate.

His combat style emphasizes:

- positioning;
- timing;
- reading opponents;
- exploiting environments;
- prepared tools;
- controlled supernatural ability;
- feints;
- teamwork;
- forcing opponents into choices.

He becomes dangerous because he turns a battlefield into a problem he has already started solving.

However, Ren's greatest weakness is that completely unpredictable opponents can disrupt his plans.

That makes Gopal especially frustrating for him.

---

# 17. REN'S POWER DEVELOPMENT

Ren does not receive a legendary shortcut.

His strength is earned over years.

He learns the world's first supernatural layer.

Later he develops the deeper spiritual layer, but in a different manner from Gopal.

Gopal's power development should be explosive and mysterious.

Ren's should be controlled and refined.

This gives us a useful contrast:

Gopal discovers what he can become.

Ren decides what he is willing to become.

---

# 18. REN'S POWER CEILING

Ren should eventually become one of the strongest non-final-boss characters.

But Gopal remains the greater individual combat force.

Not by an absurd gap.

The difference should be contextual.

In a chaotic one-on-one situation:

Gopal has the advantage.

In a planned conflict involving terrain, allies, objectives and information:

Ren can outperform almost anyone.

This allows Ren to defeat an elite warrior of the ultimate antagonist without diminishing Gopal.

---

# 19. REN'S ELITE-WARRIOR VICTORY

The fight should happen before the final battle.

Ren fights one of the ultimate antagonist's elite warriors alone or with limited assistance.

The opponent initially believes Ren is simply another subordinate leader.

Ren loses the first phase.

His plan collapses.

His group is unable to help.

Ren is injured.

Then he stops trying to fight the opponent according to the opponent's rules.

He changes the battlefield.

He uses everything he learned over the missing years.

He wins.

The victory is not a new transformation.

It is the payoff of Ren's entire character architecture.

Afterward, the elite warrior's defeat changes the world's perception of Ren.

He is no longer "the man connected to Gopal."

He has his own legend.

---

# 20. REN'S REPUTATION BEFORE REUNION

Before Gopal discovers Ren is alive, Gopal hears stories about an unknown figure.

Different people describe him differently.

"An explorer."

"A criminal."

"A protector."

"A thief."

"A negotiator."

"A dangerous information broker."

"A man who keeps appearing where governments don't want him."

Gopal never realizes who the stories are about.

This creates opportunities for subtle foreshadowing.

Readers may even encounter Ren indirectly long before the reunion.

---

# 21. REN'S RELATIONSHIP WITH GOPAL AFTER REUNION

The first phase is awkward.

They remember each other's childhood selves.

But they are adults now.

Gopal expects Ren to understand him.

Ren expects Gopal to be the same idiot.

Neither expectation is completely correct.

They argue.

They compete.

They laugh.

They accidentally recreate childhood habits.

They discover new habits in each other.

They meet each other's friends.

They dislike some of each other's friends.

Eventually they realize:

They don't need to recover the old friendship.

They can build a new one.

---

# 22. THE REUNION SHOULD NOT SOLVE EVERYTHING

This is essential.

Ren should not immediately tell Gopal everything he learned.

Gopal should not immediately tell Ren everything he experienced.

There should be information they withheld for different reasons.

This creates a short but meaningful period where the reunion is joyful but incomplete.

Eventually they confront each other.

Ren:

"You kept chasing the place without knowing what was waiting for you."

Gopal:

"And you kept searching for answers without asking what you wanted after you found them."

Both are right.

Both are wrong.

---

# 23. REN'S VIEW OF GOPAL'S CREW

Ren is initially suspicious.

Not because he dislikes them.

Because he knows what it means to follow Gopal.

He sees how much danger Gopal attracts.

He asks:

"Do they know what they're getting into?"

Gopal:

"Probably not."

Ren:

"Then you're still an idiot."

This becomes a running joke.

Eventually Ren learns that Gopal's friends are not following Gopal blindly.

They chose him.

That changes Ren's view of Gopal.

---

# 24. GOPAL'S VIEW OF REN'S GROUP

Gopal initially thinks Ren's people are too serious.

He keeps trying to make them laugh.

They think he is unbearable.

Eventually some of them love him.

Others remain suspicious.

One might eventually become a close friend of one of Gopal's companions.

This allows the two groups to develop relationships independent of Gopal and Ren.

---

# 25. THE REUNION SAGA

The reunion should happen well before the final saga.

Approximate overall placement:

75–85% of the complete story.

It should have enough space for:

1. discovery;
2. disbelief;
3. confirmation;
4. emotional reunion;
5. meeting each other's groups;
6. old habits returning;
7. disagreement;
8. Ren revealing part of his journey;
9. Gopal revealing part of his;
10. the childhood evidence resurfacing;
11. realization that both have been approaching the same destination;
12. a major external threat forcing cooperation;
13. choosing to continue together.

The reunion is not a reward.

It becomes a new phase of the story.

---

# 26. REN AND THE LEGENDARY PLACE

Ren's connection to the place should be different from Gopal's.

Gopal is emotionally connected to it.

Ren is historically connected to it.

Gopal remembers what they promised.

Ren remembers what happened because of the promise.

This difference becomes extremely important when they finally approach the destination.

---

# 27. THE FINAL JOURNEY TOGETHER

They should not immediately reach the legendary place after reunion.

They need to spend time traveling together again.

This is where we finally get to see:

Childhood Gopal + Childhood Ren
versus
Adult Gopal + Adult Ren.

They discover that their old dynamic still works.

But now both have changed enough to surprise each other.

This should contain comedy, emotional scenes, disagreements, battles, quiet conversations and discoveries.

The reader gets to enjoy them as friends again before the final revelation.

---

# 28. THEIR FINAL SYMMETRY

Gopal's journey:

A boy who acted because he cared
→ becomes someone who understands responsibility.

Ren's journey:

A boy who needed answers
→ becomes someone who understands that people matter more than answers.

Gopal learns restraint.

Ren learns trust.

Gopal learns that not every problem needs him.

Ren learns that sometimes someone has to act before certainty arrives.

Neither becomes the other.

They become better versions of themselves.

---

# 29. WHAT REN IS NOT

Ren is NOT:

- a second Gopal;
- Gopal's rival for the legendary place;
- a secret chosen one;
- a dead-person replacement;
- a permanent rescue target;
- an exposition machine;
- a power-up delivery mechanism;
- the "smart guy" with no emotional life;
- Gopal's subordinate;
- the ultimate villain's secret son;
- secretly stronger than Gopal for shock value.

---

# 30. THE EMOTIONAL CORE

The deepest idea behind Ren is:

> Two children made a promise before they understood what promises cost.

Life separated them.

They became different people.

They built different families.

They fought different battles.

They learned different truths.

And yet, when they finally meet again, one small part of them is still standing in the same place where they made that promise.

That is why the reunion matters.

Not because Ren is a plot device.

Because **time passed, people changed, and somehow the friendship survived.**

---

# 31. FINAL-SAGA ROLE

Ren enters the final saga as an independent major force.

His organization has its own role.

His people have their own battles.

Ren personally confronts one of the ultimate antagonist's elite warriors.

He survives.

He defeats them.

Then he joins Gopal for the final movement toward the legendary place.

Gopal and Ren fight side-by-side again.

But the final battle should not make Ren irrelevant.

He has his own objective inside the final conflict.

Gopal has his.

Their objectives overlap.

Neither exists merely to assist the other.

---

# 32. THE LAST PROMISE

Near the end, after everything has happened, Ren can finally bring the childhood promise back.

Not as a dramatic speech.

Something simple.

Ren:

"We said we'd go together."

Gopal:

"We're still going."

Ren:

"Good."

Then they continue.

The importance of the scene comes from the fact that the reader has spent hundreds of chapters waiting for those two children to finally finish something they started.

---

# 33. CHARACTER EQUATION

GOPAL:
Possibility → Action → Relationship → Responsibility

REN:
Question → Understanding → Trust → Responsibility

Together:
Possibility + Understanding

That combination should be one of the emotional and thematic pillars of the manga.

---

# 34. OPEN QUESTIONS WE SHOULD NOT ANSWER YET

Keep these intentionally unresolved:

- Ren's final name.
- Exact evidence the boys discovered.
- Exact identity of the abductors.
- Exact reason the evidence matters.
- Who helped Ren escape.
- Exact composition of Ren's group.
- Ren's final supernatural ability.
- The elite warrior he eventually defeats.
- Exact legendary place.
- Exact legendary event.
- What Ren ultimately discovers about the place.
- Whether Ren's independent dream changes after reaching it.
- The full reason Gopal survived while Ren was taken.

These should be solved together with the legendary-place architecture rather than independently.

---

# 35. DESIGN TEST

Ren passes the independence test if the answer to these questions is "yes":

Could Ren carry 100 chapters without Gopal? YES.

Could Ren have meaningful relationships unrelated to Gopal? YES.

Could Ren have a goal unrelated to Gopal? YES.

Could Ren disagree with Gopal and still love him? YES.

Could Ren defeat a major enemy without Gopal? YES.

Could Ren's group continue if Ren disappeared? YES.

Would Ren still matter if the legendary place didn't exist? YES.

Would Ren's life still be meaningful if Gopal never returned? YES.

Would Gopal's story lose something enormous if Ren never returned? YES.

That is the standard we should keep.

---

## CURRENT STATUS

Ren is now a major PROVISIONAL character pillar.

The next design dependency is not another chapter.

It is:

**THE CHILDHOOD INCIDENT**

We need to determine exactly what Gopal and Ren discovered, why it was impossible, who noticed them, why Ren was taken, and how that incident connects to the legendary place.

Only after that should we finalize Ren's group, Ren's power, and the legendary destination.
