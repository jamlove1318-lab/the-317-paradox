# CHAPTER 2 — THE GIRL WHO HAD SOMEWHERE TO BE

Status: PROVISIONAL — REBUILT OPENING DRAFT

## Page 1

By noon, Gopal had learned three things.

The first was that hills looked smaller from far away.

The second was that a person could become hungry much faster than expected.

The third was that a shoe could be surprisingly useful when thrown at a bird.

He missed the bird.

It stole half his bread.

Gopal watched it fly away.

"You didn't even need it."

The bird did not apologize.

Gopal ate the other half.

## Page 2

The first settlement beyond Veyra was called Bellwater.

Gopal had expected something enormous.

It was six streets.

A bridge.

A bakery.

A stable.

A little square.

And a bell hanging from a tower that rang whenever somebody crossed the bridge.

Gopal stood beneath it.

"Why?"

A baker carrying flour answered without looking at him.

"Because that's what it does."

"Who made it?"

"Someone."

"Why?"

"Because people cross bridges."

"Why does the bridge need a bell?"

The baker stopped.

He looked at Gopal.

"You've been outside Veyra for one day, haven't you?"

"Yes."

"I can tell."

## Page 3

Gopal bought bread.

He had money.

He was pleased to discover this.

He had also forgotten that money could run out.

The baker gave him a suspicious look.

"You traveling alone?"

"Yes."

"Family know?"

"Yes."

"Do they know you're carrying one shoe?"

Gopal looked down.

"Apparently everyone notices that."

The baker sighed.

"There's a room above the stable. Cheap."

Gopal brightened.

"Really?"

"If you help clean the stalls."

"Done."

"You haven't seen them."

"I've seen horses."

"These aren't horses."

Gopal paused.

"How bad?"

The baker smiled.

"Now you're learning."

## Page 4

The stable contained two horses.

One donkey.

And a creature Gopal could not identify.

It had four legs.

Mostly.

One was considerably shorter.

It stared at him.

Gopal stared back.

"Hello."

It sneezed.

Gopal stepped backward.

"Fair."

A voice behind him said:

"You've never cleaned a stable."

Gopal turned.

A girl stood in the doorway.

She wore a patched traveling coat and carried a long case of tools.

Her hair was tied badly.

Her boots were excellent.

Gopal looked at the boots.

Then at his one shoe.

She noticed.

"Don't."

"I wasn't going to say anything."

"You were thinking it."

"I was thinking your boots look useful."

She blinked.

"That's somehow worse."

## Page 5

Her name was **Savi**.

She was seventeen.

She was traveling east.

She was apprenticed to a bridgewright in a coastal city called Merrow.

She wanted to build bridges.

Not beautiful bridges.

Useful ones.

Bridges that survived floods.

Bridges that could be repaired by ordinary people.

Bridges that didn't collapse because somebody wanted them to look impressive.

Gopal liked her immediately.

Savi did not like Gopal immediately.

"Why are you traveling?"

"To see the world."

"That's not a destination."

"I know."

"Then where are you going?"

"I don't know."

She stared at him.

"You really are from Veyra."

## Page 6

Gopal told her about the road.

Not the river.

Not the strange mark.

Just the road.

Savi told him about Merrow.

She had never been there.

Her master had accepted her as an apprentice through a letter.

She had six months to arrive.

"If I miss the date, someone else gets the place."

"So you have to hurry."

"Yes."

Gopal nodded solemnly.

"I will help you."

"No."

"I haven't said how."

"Exactly."

## Page 7

The stable creature screamed.

Everyone froze.

Gopal dropped his brush.

Savi grabbed her tool case.

The donkey kicked the wall.

Outside, somebody shouted.

"What was that?"

Gopal whispered:

"Your horse."

"It's not a horse."

"I know."

The creature screamed again.

A moment later, a stable door burst open.

Three goats ran into the street.

The baker screamed.

A child laughed.

Gopal ran after the goats.

Savi ran after Gopal.

Neither knew why.

## Page 8

They caught the first goat near the bakery.

The second jumped over Gopal.

The third entered the bell tower.

The bell rang.

The entire town looked up.

Gopal pointed.

"That's impressive."

Savi grabbed his collar.

"Stop admiring it."

They climbed the tower.

The goat stood beside the bell rope.

Gopal approached slowly.

"Hello."

The goat stared.

"Please don't."

The goat pulled the rope.

DONG.

Gopal jumped.

Savi covered her ears.

DONG.

"Why does this keep happening?"

DONG.

Gopal whispered:

"I think it likes the sound."

Savi looked at him.

"Do not encourage it."

## Page 9

They eventually got the goat down.

The baker gave them both free bread.

Savi looked at Gopal.

"You attract problems."

"I attract interesting things."

"Those are not the same."

Gopal thought about it.

"Sometimes they are."

Savi almost smiled.

Almost.

Then she noticed his notebook.

"You write everything down?"

"Things I don't understand."

"That's a lot."

"Yes."

She pointed at the page.

"What's that?"

Gopal had drawn the painted piece of wood.

The mark was small.

Savi's expression changed.

Only slightly.

Gopal noticed.

"You know it?"

"No."

She answered too quickly.

## Page 10

Gopal waited.

Savi looked away.

"I've seen something similar."

"Where?"

"On a bridge."

"When?"

"Three years ago."

"Which bridge?"

"An old one."

"Where?"

She sighed.

"You ask too many questions."

"I know."

"It wasn't the same mark."

"Almost?"

"Maybe."

Gopal closed the notebook.

He didn't press.

Savi looked surprised.

"You're not asking?"

"You don't want to tell me."

"That's right."

"Okay."

She studied him.

"That's it?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Gopal shrugged.

"Because it's yours."

## Page 11

The answer stayed between them.

Savi looked at the road outside.

"My master told me something."

"What?"

"Never build a bridge because someone tells you where it should go."

Gopal smiled.

"That sounds wise."

"It's annoying."

"Most wise things are."

She laughed.

It escaped before she could stop it.

Gopal grinned.

"You laughed."

"I did not."

"You did."

"I coughed."

"That was definitely a laugh."

Savi threw a piece of bread at him.

Gopal caught it.

"Thank you."

## Page 12

That evening, Gopal discovered the room above the stable was not a room.

It was a storage loft with one blanket.

He stared at the blanket.

Savi looked over his shoulder.

"You wanted adventure."

"This is storage."

"You wanted the world."

"The world is poorly furnished."

She laughed again.

This time she didn't deny it.

Gopal sat down.

Savi opened her tool case.

Inside were hammers, measuring cords, clamps and strange metal pieces.

"You really want to build bridges?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

She didn't answer immediately.

"When I was little, the river flooded our village."

Gopal waited.

"The bridge broke."

"Did someone die?"

"No."

She looked at him.

"That's why I remember it."

Gopal didn't understand.

She continued.

"Everyone survived. But for six days, half the village couldn't reach the other half."

She touched one of the tools.

"I decided I wanted to build things that people could trust."

## Page 13

Gopal looked at his notebook.

Then at Savi.

"You should build one for Veyra."

"Maybe."

"Come back someday."

"Maybe."

"I'll help."

"You don't know anything about bridges."

"I can learn."

Savi smiled.

"You're strange."

"So I've been told."

## Page 14

The next morning, Savi left early.

Gopal found her beside the road.

"You didn't say goodbye."

"I haven't left."

"You're standing beside the road."

"So?"

"That's where people leave."

"I'm waiting for a wagon."

"To Merrow?"

"Yes."

Gopal looked down the eastern road.

"That's my direction too."

Savi frowned.

"You said you didn't know where you were going."

"I still don't."

"Then don't follow me."

"I'm not."

"Good."

A wagon approached.

Savi climbed aboard.

Gopal watched.

Then the wagon stopped.

The driver shouted:

"One passenger only!"

Savi looked back.

Gopal shrugged.

"See you somewhere."

The wagon continued.

## Page 15

Gopal walked west.

He lasted eleven minutes.

Then he turned around.

Not because he was following Savi.

Because he had forgotten his hammer.

He returned to Bellwater.

Savi was still there.

The wagon had broken an axle.

She was furious.

Gopal looked at the broken wheel.

Then at his hammer.

Then at her.

"I can help."

Savi crossed her arms.

"Can you?"

Gopal smiled.

"Probably."

## Page 16

The wagon driver had tried to repair the axle with rope.

Savi said it would not hold.

Gopal said she was right.

The driver asked if they had a better idea.

They didn't.

For the first time, Gopal saw Savi stop pretending she knew what to do.

She looked worried.

"I'll miss the next wagon."

"Then we repair this one."

"We don't have the right wood."

Gopal looked around.

Bellwater had a bridge.

The bridge had old repair planks.

Savi immediately shook her head.

"No."

Gopal understood.

"We can't take from the bridge."

"Exactly."

He smiled.

"Then we'll find another way."

## Page 17

They spent the afternoon searching.

Old fence.

Broken cart.

Discarded beams.

Nothing strong enough.

Gopal wanted to give up.

Savi didn't.

At sunset, they found an abandoned handcart behind the mill.

Its axle was the right size.

Savi inspected it.

"Perfect."

Gopal grinned.

"Can we take it?"

"No."

"Oh."

"We can ask."

They asked.

The miller said yes.

The wagon was repaired before dark.

Savi's departure was delayed by one day.

She wasn't happy.

But the driver was.

The baker was.

The goats were indifferent.

Gopal was exhausted.

## Page 18

Savi handed Gopal a small metal clamp.

"Keep this."

"What for?"

"You're going to break something eventually."

"I already have tools."

"You have a hammer."

"And a spoon."

"That's not a tool."

"It can be."

She shook her head.

Then she became serious.

"You were going to use the bridge planks."

Gopal looked away.

"Maybe."

"You would've damaged the bridge."

"I know."

"But you stopped."

"You told me not to."

"No."

She smiled slightly.

"You stopped because you understood."

Gopal looked at the clamp.

"Is that good?"

"It's a start."

## Page 19

Savi left the next morning.

This time, Gopal didn't follow.

He stood beside the road.

She climbed into the wagon.

Then she leaned out.

"Hey, Gopal."

"Yes?"

"You're still going the wrong direction."

Gopal looked at the road.

He laughed.

"I know."

"Where are you going?"

He looked toward the hills.

"Somewhere."

Savi smiled.

"That's still not a destination."

"I'll work on it."

The wagon rolled away.

Gopal watched until it disappeared.

He opened his notebook.

**Day 2.**

**Met Savi.**

**She wants to build bridges.**

He paused.

Then wrote:

**I think I want to see where hers lead.**

## Page 20

That evening, Gopal reached the next crossroads.

Three roads.

Again.

He sighed.

"Really?"

A weathered sign pointed toward the northern road.

**ORRIN — 2 DAYS**

The eastern road:

**MERROW — 11 DAYS**

The southern road:

**RIVER MARKET — TOMORROW**

Gopal looked at the three signs.

He remembered Savi.

Then he remembered the mark on the painted wood.

He reached into his bag.

The wooden piece was warm.

Gopal stopped.

He did not smile.

He simply looked at it.

Then he put it away.

"Not yet."

He chose the southern road.

The market came first.

The mystery could wait.

For once, Gopal chose the road because of a person, not a question.
