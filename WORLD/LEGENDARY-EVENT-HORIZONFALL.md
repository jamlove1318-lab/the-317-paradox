# LEGENDARY EVENT — PROVISIONAL DESIGN

> Status: PROVISIONAL
> This document advances the legendary-event architecture from a blank slate into a working candidate.
> No final name is locked.

## 1. THE EVENT

Working title:

# THE HORIZONFALL

Centuries ago, the world experienced an event that no civilization can completely explain.

For one day, horizons around the world stopped behaving like horizons.

People in distant places saw the **same impossible landscape** beyond their own horizon.

Sailors saw it beyond the sea.

Mountain villages saw it beyond mountain ridges.

Desert caravans saw it beyond dunes.

People looking through high towers saw it.

The image was not a cloud or mirage.

It had physical detail.

Different civilizations recorded the same impossible features.

Then, for a short period, the impossible landscape became reachable.

A small number of people attempted to cross toward it.

Most disappeared.

One expedition is confirmed to have reached it.

That expedition became the origin of the worldwide legendary-place pursuit.

---

## 2. WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT FROM A SIMPLE "MYSTERIOUS ISLAND"

The phenomenon does not merely reveal a hidden island.

It temporarily changes the relationship between **where a person is** and **where the destination can be reached from**.

The legendary place is therefore not simply hidden.

It is normally outside ordinary geographical accessibility.

During the event, the world briefly aligned with it.

This is the seed of the later discovery:

> The destination is real, but ordinary geography cannot describe its relationship to the world.

The final explanation remains unknown.

---

# 3. WHAT ORDINARY PEOPLE EXPERIENCED

The event becomes legendary because almost everyone experienced something.

A farmer looking across a field saw an unfamiliar coastline.

A child saw enormous structures beyond the village.

A fisherman saw a mountain rising where open water should have been.

A city saw the same impossible moonlit architecture that another civilization thousands of kilometers away recorded.

People drew what they saw.

Songs were written.

Religions formed around interpretations.

Governments tried to control the routes.

Markets crashed and changed.

Navigation systems were rewritten.

Some communities still celebrate the anniversary.

Some cultures mourn those who disappeared.

Some consider the event a blessing.

Others call it a disaster.

---

# 4. THE IMPOSSIBLE DETAIL

Every surviving historical record agrees on one detail:

### The landscape was recognizable across civilizations.

Different languages described different things, but the physical features match.

This creates one of the world's oldest questions:

> How could people who had never met see the same place?

This is one reason the legendary destination became more than a travel story.

It became a civilization-wide question.

---

# 5. THE FIRST EXPEDITIONS

Once the event ended, people began searching for the place.

Explorers tried to reconstruct the routes.

Governments funded expeditions.

Religious groups sent pilgrims.

Criminal organizations searched for hidden routes.

Scholars compared records.

Treasure hunters fabricated maps.

Entire professions eventually formed around the pursuit.

The legendary place becomes a cultural obsession rather than a niche mystery.

---

# 6. THE RETURNING EXPEDITION

One expedition eventually claims to have reached the place.

Their leader becomes one of the most famous people in history.

The expedition disappears from ordinary records for a period of time.

Then their vessel returns.

The people aboard are alive.

They have physical evidence.

But their account is incomplete.

Different members remember different things.

Some refuse to speak.

Some say the place exists.

Some insist they never reached a place at all.

One surviving statement becomes famous:

> "We did not find the horizon. The horizon found us."

This exact line is PROVISIONAL and may be replaced.

The important idea is that reaching the destination did not produce a clean answer.

It produced more disagreement.

---

# 7. THE EVENT'S AFTERMATH

The world changes because of the event.

### Navigation
New navigation traditions emerge.

### Architecture
Some cities build towers aligned with the direction in which the horizon appeared.

### Religion
Some religions interpret the event as divine.

### Scholarship
Universities establish entire fields around studying it.

### Commerce
Maps, expedition equipment, guidebooks, fake relics and "authentic" routes become businesses.

### Crime
Smugglers sell supposed access routes.

### Politics
Governments restrict certain historical sites and routes.

### Tourism
Some places become famous because their ancestors supposedly saw the phenomenon.

### Festivals
Annual celebrations reenact the horizon's appearance.

### Children's culture
Children play games about reaching the impossible horizon.

This means the legendary event exists in daily life even when the main story is nowhere near its mystery.

---

# 8. DIFFERENT CIVILIZATIONS REMEMBER IT DIFFERENTLY

This is important.

There is no single official historical version.

One civilization says:

> The horizon came from the sea.

Another says:

> It came from the sky.

Another says:

> The world briefly became transparent.

Another says:

> The destination was always there and the world moved.

Another says:

> The event never happened and the records are fabricated.

All of these traditions contain fragments of truth.

None contains the whole picture.

---

# 9. WHAT THE WORLD CALLS THE DESTINATION

We should NOT finalize its name yet.

Different cultures should have different names.

The global common name can develop later.

This gives the destination multiple identities:

### Public identity
The legendary place everyone wants to reach.

### Historical identity
The place connected to Horizonfall.

### Actual identity
Something stranger that cannot be described accurately by the old names.

---

# 10. THE CHILDHOOD CONNECTION

This event connects directly to Gopal and Ren's childhood incident.

The evidence they discover is a tiny surviving object/record associated with Horizonfall.

The boys don't understand its significance.

But Ren notices something important:

The impossible image they see is not merely an image.

It behaves according to the same strange geographical rule described in ancient Horizonfall records.

That is why someone becomes interested in what they found.

---

# 11. WHY SOMEONE WOULD ABDUCT REN

The people who notice the discovery already know that Horizonfall was not merely a historical spectacle.

They are trying to control information about the destination.

Ren's memory is therefore valuable.

He remembers a detail that could potentially help identify when or where the phenomenon can happen again.

The abductors do not need to understand the entire truth.

They only need to know:

> "This child saw something we have spent generations trying to find."

That is enough.

---

# 12. THE IMPORTANT HISTORICAL QUESTION

Horizonfall is not the only time the phenomenon may have happened.

Ancient records contain hints of earlier occurrences.

But historians disagree about whether those references describe:

- the same event;
- different events;
- religious metaphors;
- deliberately falsified history;
- or a recurring property of the destination.

This creates a long-term mystery.

---

# 13. THE ULTIMATE ANTAGONIST CONNECTION

The ultimate antagonist's organization has inherited information about Horizonfall.

They know more than most governments.

But they do not necessarily know everything.

This prevents the villain from becoming an omniscient exposition machine.

Their organization has spent generations collecting:

- expedition records;
- forbidden maps;
- witness accounts;
- artifacts;
- oral histories;
- failed navigation methods;
- information from captured explorers.

The antagonist wants the destination for a reason that will eventually intersect with Gopal's true dream.

That reason is NOT finalized yet.

---

# 14. THE EVENT'S BIGGER PURPOSE

Horizonfall should eventually answer one question:

> **Why could the world see the same impossible place?**

But answering it should create a much bigger question:

> **Why does this place have a relationship with the world at all?**

And that should eventually connect to the deepest history of the setting.

---

# 15. FAIR MYSTERY DESIGN

The reader should be able to look back and realize:

The truth was always visible.

We should plant:

- matching historical drawings;
- repeated symbols;
- contradictory maps;
- old buildings aligned toward impossible directions;
- festivals whose rituals preserve distorted information;
- children’s stories containing accurate details;
- navigation traditions that seem superstitious;
- old expedition journals;
- characters who casually mention unusual geography;
- Ren's childhood memory;
- Gopal's incomplete memory.

The reveal should recontextualize old information rather than inventing a completely new rule at the end.

---

# 16. THE SPECTACLE

The original Horizonfall sequence should eventually be shown visually.

Imagine:

A normal ocean.

Ships sailing.

People working.

Children playing.

Then every horizon begins to change.

Not explosively.

Quietly.

The distant line of the world bends.

Something appears beyond it.

A coastline that cannot exist.

A mountain where there is no land.

A city whose architecture no known civilization recognizes.

People across the world look toward their horizon at the same moment.

And somewhere, far away:

Two ordinary children will one day find a tiny remnant of that impossible event.

---

# 17. WHY THE EVENT MATTERS TO THE WHOLE MANGA

Horizonfall is not just lore.

It creates:

- exploration;
- history;
- politics;
- religion;
- commerce;
- crime;
- scholarship;
- festivals;
- myths;
- careers;
- rivalries;
- scams;
- wars;
- family traditions;
- childhood dreams;
- government restrictions;
- international competition.

This lets the legendary place influence the world without requiring every chapter to discuss it.

---

# 18. IMPORTANT LIMIT

The event is NOT the story.

Gopal can spend hundreds of chapters eating food, making friends, fighting local criminals, attending festivals, getting into ridiculous situations, discovering cultures, helping communities, losing battles, learning powers, and dealing with ordinary problems.

Horizonfall may be nowhere in the foreground.

But it remains part of the civilization underneath the story.

# 21. NEXT DEPENDENCY

The next major design should be:

## THE LEGENDARY PLACE ITSELF

Now that we have a historical event, we can ask:

> If this place caused Horizonfall, what is it actually like?

It must be more than a location.

It must have:

- its own ecology;
- geography;
- civilization/history;
- unique physical rule;
- inhabitants or traces of inhabitants;
- dangers;
- beauty;
- ordinary life;
- things Gopal can experience;
- things Ren understands differently;
- something impossible that cannot exist elsewhere;
- a reason the world has never simply conquered it;
- a reason reaching it changes the story rather than ending it.

The destination should feel like a place readers would want to explore even if they knew nothing about its mystery.
