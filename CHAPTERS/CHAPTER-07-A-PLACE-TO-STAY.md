# Chapter 07 — A Place to Stay

**Status:** PROVISIONAL — STORY DRAFT
**Saga:** 01 — The First Horizon

## 24-page manga script

### Page 01 — Morning Work
Gopal wakes to the cook shouting his name. He answers that he is awake. The cook replies that being awake isn't the same as moving.

### Page 02 — Kitchen
The cook gives him vegetables to prepare. Gopal asks how many. The answer is “enough.” He discovers that ordinary work rarely comes with adventure-style instructions.

### Page 03 — Learning
He carries water, washes bowls, folds sheets, delivers breakfast and collects dishes. He is clumsy but genuinely trying.

### Page 04 — Travelers
At breakfast he listens to travelers discuss students, broken bridges, relatives, weather and work. None of them are heroes; all have lives.

### Page 05 — House Rules
The cook lists rules: don't waste food, don't wake guests, don't touch bags, don't start fights, don't jump into the river. Gopal raises a hand. The cook says no before hearing the question.

### Page 06 — Laundry Ghost
A sheet wraps around Gopal while he hangs laundry. A child calls him a ghost. Gopal plays along and scares the child, then gets scolded for it.

### Page 07 — Children
The boarding-house children ask whether Gopal has fought monsters. He hasn't. When asked why he left Veyra, he simply says he wanted to see.

### Page 08 — Small Stories
Travelers tell stories about mountains, failed marriages and a shoe lost on a ferry. The children laugh hardest at the shoe story. Gopal realizes a life doesn't need to be legendary to be worth hearing.

### Page 09 — Market Errand
The cook sends Gopal to buy vegetables and explicitly tells him to return with the list, not with whatever he happens to find interesting.

### Page 10 — Distraction
Gopal stops for a street performer, a dog, a mechanical toy and a wrestling match. He finally looks at his empty basket and remembers the vegetables.

### Page 11 — Consequence
He returns late. Half the vegetables are wrong. He claims half is pretty good. The cook informs him that half is not dinner.

### Page 12 — Bran Visits
Bran eats at the boarding house and notices Gopal working. Gopal calls the job temporary. Bran says everything is temporary. The line quietly echoes the theme of travel without becoming exposition.

### Page 13 — River Trade
Bran explains how boats, families, ferries, private routes and markets depend on one another. Some routes are cheap; some are dangerous; some are avoided for reasons Gopal doesn't understand.

### Page 14 — The Question He Doesn't Ask
Bran mentions a northern road. A nearby merchant becomes quiet. Gopal notices, almost asks, then remembers the lesson about not solving everything he sees. He lets the question go.

### Page 15 — Small Victory
The cook gives Gopal another task. He completes it correctly. The cook simply says, “Better.” Gopal is disproportionately proud.

### Page 16 — Home Message
Rajan writes about repairing a cart. Mira writes about the weather. Nemi again asks whether Gopal is eating enough. Gopal laughs.

### Page 17 — Reply
Gopal writes: “I am eating. I have a place to stay. Aster Reach is huge.” Then he adds, “I wish you could see it.”

### Page 18 — Quiet Balcony
Gopal sits above the river. The city hums below. He recognizes sounds that were strangers a few days ago.

### Page 19 — Familiarity
He knows where the water bucket is, which stair creaks, which window sticks and which child steals fruit. Familiarity has begun without him noticing.

### Page 20 — Not Smaller
Aster Reach has not become smaller. Gopal has simply gained points of reference. One street becomes known, then one shop, one dock, one face.

### Page 21 — The Notice
Outside the boarding house is a survey notice: “ROUTE SURVEY NEEDED — NORTH RIVER ROAD.” Gopal reads it twice. He doesn't investigate.

### Page 22 — Choice
He almost asks about it. He doesn't. This is a tiny but genuine act of self-control.

### Page 23 — River
That evening he watches boats pass. He wonders where they are going but does not chase them. Curiosity is still there; judgment is beginning to grow beside it.

### Page 24 — Enough
The boarding-house lights come on. Someone calls Gopal inside. He answers automatically and goes. Final line: “For now, this was enough.”

## Continuity
The boarding house is now an emotional anchor. Gopal has begun belonging to a place without giving up his desire to travel. The north-road survey notice naturally leads to Sera, who is introduced through work rather than mystery.
